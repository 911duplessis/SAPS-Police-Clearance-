Common mistakes that delay a SAPS police clearance application

Every one of these is avoidable. Most of them cost applicants weeks — here's what to check before you submit.

The List

Eight mistakes worth checking for

1. Poor-quality fingerprints

+

By far the most common cause of rejection. Smudged, partial or incorrectly captured prints on the wrong form get an entire application sent back — meaning weeks of processing time lost and a full re-submission. This is why fingerprint capture technique matters more than almost anything else in the file. See our fingerprint requirements guide.

2. Inconsistent name spelling across documents

+

If your ID, application form and cover letter don't spell your name identically — including middle names, maiden names, or accents — it can flag a mismatch. Double-check every document uses the same spelling before submission.

3. Assuming the certificate is instantly reusable for a different purpose

+

A certificate obtained for one immigration application isn't automatically accepted for a different one months later if the receiving authority requires it to be recent. Check the age requirement for your specific use case before assuming an existing certificate will do.

4. Not checking whether an apostille is required before applying

+

Discovering partway through a visa deadline that your certificate needed to be apostilled — and that this takes additional time on top of the original application — is one of the most stressful and avoidable delays. Check upfront. See our apostille guide.

5. Leaving it until the deadline is close

+

SAPS's own internal processing time is the part nobody — not SAPS, not any coordinator, anywhere in the world — can expedite. Starting early is the only real lever you have over your own timeline.

6. Using an uncertified or inexperienced fingerprint provider to save money

+

A cheaper fingerprint appointment that results in a rejected submission ends up costing more in time than it saved in fees — often the difference between a smooth 4–6 week process and a much longer one.

7. Providing an address that can't reliably receive an international courier

+

Your finished certificate is a physical document that has to be couriered back. An address that's hard to deliver to, or a house you're about to move out of, can cause real delays at the very last step.

8. Not asking questions when something is unclear

+

SAPS requirements are precise, and getting one detail wrong on a form is enough to cause a delay. If anything about your application is unclear, ask before submitting rather than guessing.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked

What's the single most common reason applications get delayed?

+

Fingerprint quality, by a wide margin. A print that's smudged, incomplete, or captured on the wrong form gets an entire application sent back for re-submission, adding weeks.

Can a mistake in my application get it rejected outright, or just delayed?

+

Most issues result in the application being returned for correction rather than a hard rejection — but a return-and-resubmit cycle still costs real time, since it goes back through the same courier and processing steps.

If I already made one of these mistakes with a previous application, can it be fixed?

+

Usually, yes — by correcting the issue and resubmitting. If you're dealing with a returned or rejected application, get in touch and Elanza can advise on the fastest path from where you currently are.

Does using a coordination service actually reduce these risks?

+

The value is specifically in catching these issues before submission — checking documents are consistent, fingerprints meet the standard, and nothing obvious is missing — rather than finding out after SAPS sends the file back.
