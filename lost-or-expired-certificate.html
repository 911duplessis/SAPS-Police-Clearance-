Lost or expired SAPS Police Clearance Certificate

What actually happens next — and why there's no shortcut back to a certificate you already had.

The Short Answer

There's no reissue — only a fresh application

Unlike a lost ID document, a SAPS Police Clearance Certificate can't be reprinted or reissued from a copy on file. If yours is lost, damaged, or simply too old for what a receiving authority now requires, the only path forward is a brand-new application — new fingerprints, a new submission to SAPS, and the same processing time as the first time around. There's no way to skip or shortcut this because SAPS doesn't keep a "certificate on file" to reissue; each certificate reflects a check run at the time of that specific application.

Two Different Situations

Lost vs. simply too old

Genuinely Lost or Damaged

The physical certificate is missing, destroyed, or otherwise unusable. There's no digital reissue — a full new application is required, regardless of how recently the original was issued.

Still Have It, But It's Too Old

The certificate itself is fine, but the authority asking for it now wants one issued more recently than yours. This is extremely common when applying for a second visa, a new job, or a different country's immigration process months or years after your first certificate.

Check the actual requirement first: before assuming you need a new certificate, confirm the specific recency window the authority you're dealing with actually requires — see our explainer on certificate validity. Sometimes an existing certificate is still acceptable.
Starting Again

What a fresh application involves

Practically, reapplying is identical to applying the first time: new certified fingerprints, a completed application form, and the same supporting documents — see our required documents checklist and fingerprint requirements guide. The good news is you already know what to expect, and there's nothing about a previous certificate that complicates a new one — SAPS treats it as a standard new request.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked

Can SAPS reissue my certificate from a copy if I've lost the original?

+

No. There's no reissue process — a lost certificate means starting a completely new application, including new fingerprints. Keep the original somewhere safe once it arrives, and keep a scanned copy for your own records even though a scan can't be submitted in its place.

My certificate isn't lost, it's just old — do I still need a new one?

+

Usually, yes, if the authority asking for it has a recency requirement. SAPS doesn't print an expiry date, but most receiving authorities (immigration departments, employers) want one issued within roughly the last six to twelve months. An old, otherwise-valid certificate can still be rejected purely for being too old for that specific requirement.

Can I use one certificate for two different applications if they're close together?

+

Sometimes — it depends entirely on whether both receiving authorities accept the same issue date within their own recency window. Check both requirements before assuming one certificate covers two applications.

Does a new application take the same 4–6 weeks as the first one?

+

Yes — there's no fast-track around SAPS's own processing time just because you've applied before. The fingerprint capture and document preparation side can move quickly, but SAPS's internal processing is the same for a first application or a fifth one.

What if I need a certificate again but I've since moved within New Zealand?

+

That's fine — the application isn't tied to a specific New Zealand address, only to your South African ID. Update your current contact and delivery details when you apply again.
