Apostille & authentication for your SAPS certificate

When "just the certificate" isn't enough, and what legalising it for overseas use actually involves.

Why This Comes Up

A certificate needs to be trusted by someone who's never seen a real one

A SAPS Police Clearance Certificate is a genuine, verifiable South African government document — but an immigration officer or employer overseas has no easy way to independently confirm that a stamp or signature on it is real. Legalisation exists to solve exactly that problem: it's a formal, internationally recognised way of confirming a document's authenticity so it can be relied on outside the country that issued it.

Not every use case requires it — plenty of immigration processes accept the plain SAPS certificate. But when it is required, skipping it is one of the more time-costly mistakes an applicant can make, because you can't retroactively apostille a certificate quickly once you're already deep into a visa deadline.

How It Works

South Africa and the Apostille Convention

South Africa is a party to the Hague Apostille Convention, which means public documents — including SAPS certificates — issued in South Africa can generally be certified with an apostille for use in other member countries, rather than requiring full embassy-by-embassy legalisation. In practice this simplifies things considerably compared to the older consular legalisation process, but the certifying authority and exact steps depend on the document type and can be updated by the South African government from time to time.

Because legalisation requirements and processes are set by government departments and can change, treat this page as a starting orientation rather than a final procedural guide — always confirm current requirements with the receiving authority and the relevant South African department before relying on any timeline.
Common Questions

Frequently Asked

Does every SAPS Police Clearance Certificate need to be apostilled?

+

No. Whether legalisation is required depends entirely on who's asking for the certificate. Some immigration authorities and employers accept the plain SAPS certificate as-is; others specifically require it to be apostilled or otherwise authenticated. Always check the exact requirement of the receiving authority before assuming either way.

What is an apostille, in plain terms?

+

It's an internationally recognised certificate — attached to your document — confirming that the signature, seal or stamp on it is genuine. It exists so a document issued in one country can be trusted by an authority in another country that's also party to the Hague Apostille Convention, without further embassy legalisation.

Is South Africa part of the Apostille Convention?

+

Yes — South Africa formally joined the Hague Apostille Convention, which means South African public documents, including SAPS certificates, can generally be apostilled rather than needing full embassy/consular legalisation for use in other member countries. Processes and requirements can be updated, so it's worth confirming current guidance before you apply.

Who issues an apostille for a South African document?

+

In South Africa, apostilles for public documents are issued through the relevant government authority — historically via the High Court and the Department of International Relations and Cooperation (DIRCO) depending on the document type. Because procedures can change, always confirm the current process before relying on older information.

+
