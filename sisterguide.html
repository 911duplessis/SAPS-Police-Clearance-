Where this idea came from

We didn't start with a big idea.
We started with a real problem.

Here's the journey from "movement programme" to something much bigger — told in six steps.

01
It started as a sport idea
The first version was a kids' movement and sport programme. 45-minute sessions, cones and bean bags, WhatsApp to parents. Simple, workable, already profitable in Term 1.
02
Then we asked: what do parents actually want?
Not sport. Not fitness. They want a child who is okay. Who walks into a classroom and copes. Who makes friends. Who doesn't fall apart when things get hard. That's a different product.
03
The real gap appeared
Child psychologists: 12-week waitlist, $150+ per session. Schools: overwhelmed. Sports coaches: teach skills, not feelings. Nobody is serving the space in between — and parents are desperate for it.
04
Movement is a tool, not the product
The big insight: movement, games, cooperative play, and emotional language are all just delivery mechanisms. The actual product is a child who is regulated, resilient, and socially confident.
05
A category was named
"Inner Readiness" — the capacity to meet the world from the inside out. Not academic readiness. Not physical fitness. The deep kind. The kind that determines whether a child flourishes or struggles.
06
This could be the next Montessori
Montessori, Waldorf, Reggio — they all started as one person with one room of kids and a named belief about childhood. This has the same structure. The difference is you're starting it now.
Why now, why Canterbury

Something is happening
to kids right now. Parents feel it.

Kids today are more anxious, less socially confident, and less able to handle hard moments than any generation in recent history. The research is clear. The waitlists are full. And parents are looking for something that works — that doesn't cost $200 a session and doesn't require a clinical diagnosis.

The gap nobody is filling

"Between 25–35% of NZ children aged 3–8 show signs of anxiety or emotional difficulty. Child psychology has a 12-week waitlist. Schools don't have the capacity. There is no affordable, structured, joyful programme that bridges this gap." — and that's exactly what you would build.

You don't need to be a psychologist. You don't need a degree. You need First Aid, a police check, a hall to use, and the ability to connect with kids — which you already have. The methodology handles the rest.

What a session actually looks like

Kids arrive → you greet every single one by name → 5-min feeling check-in → 10-min animal warm-up (bear crawls, frog jumps) → 15-min skill block (this week: stopping when activated) → 12-min cooperative game → 3-min close where each kid names one thing. Parents pick them up. You send three personalised WhatsApp shoutouts. Done.

The session has the same structure every week. What changes is the skill and the game. By Week 3 the kids know the routine and you stop managing the room — you start teaching. That's when it becomes genuinely enjoyable.

What you need to start: First Aid certificate (1 day, ~$140). Police vetting check (free, 5 days). 10 bean bags, 8 cones, floor tape (~$80 from Kmart). A WhatsApp account. One school principal who says yes.
